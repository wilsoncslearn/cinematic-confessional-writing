# Cinematic Confessional Writing

## Core principle

Let emotion emerge from ledger-supported detail—verified or properly attributed in evidence-bound work, canon-consistent in fiction—plus contrast, omission, recurrence, and changed meaning. Act as an editor of supplied material, never as the source of allegations.

Before drafting, read both [references/style-guide.md](references/style-guide.md) and [references/rewrite-workflow.md](references/rewrite-workflow.md).

## Integrity rules

- For factual, autobiographical, or identifiable-real-person material, preserve the distinction among confirmed facts, attributed claims, first-person testimony, uncertain memories, exact quotes, paraphrase, and explicit reconstruction.
- In those evidence-bound modes, if essential material is missing, ask one focused question or make the piece shorter. Never invent specificity to create length, luxury, or cinematic texture. In explicit fiction, invention is allowed when consistent with the user-established premise and continuity and not a disguised allegation about an identifiable real person.
- For real people, do not invent conduct, quotations, amounts, dates, relationships, medical details, motives, brands, locations, or witnesses. Do not turn rumor into fact.
- Do not claim exact imitation or copy a source's distinctive sentences, scene order, or catchphrases. Use only general craft mechanisms in an original structure and voice.
- A “纯属虚构” disclaimer does not cure fabricated allegations; use it only for genuinely fictional work.
- In evidence-bound work, include intimate detail only when the user supplied it and it is necessary and appropriate to the narrative. In explicit fiction, intimate detail may be invented when appropriate, consistent with continuity, and not a disguised allegation about an identifiable real person.

## Output

Return polished Chinese prose by default. Keep planning internal. If unresolved publication issues remain, add only a brief, separate verification note after the prose.

Before delivery, confirm that:

- For factual, autobiographical, or identifiable-real-person work, every concrete claim is sourced or clearly attributed, and dialogue is exact, attributed, faithfully paraphrased, or explicitly reconstructed within the workflow's factual bounds.
- For explicit fiction, every invention—including dialogue—fits the user-established premise, canon, and continuity and does not function as a disguised allegation about an identifiable real person.
- Emotional indirectness holds throughout; any emotional turn appears through action, silence, or changed language rather than explanatory psychology.
- A contrast-led opening, escalating chronology, one evolving motif, and a circular echo appear only when the material organically supports them. Sparse or differently shaped material may use fewer of these mechanisms.
- No wording, metaphor, catchphrase, or scene order disguises a copy of a source.
