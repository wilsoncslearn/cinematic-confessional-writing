# Style Guide

## Intended effect

Keep a cool surface over emotional pressure. Let readers infer emotion from logistics, objects, choices, and silence rather than explanations of what a person supposedly felt.

The mechanisms below are an adaptable toolkit, not a mandatory formula. Select only those organically supported by the material; sparse or differently shaped accounts may need few of them. For factual, autobiographical, or identifiable-real-person material, “supported” means traceable to the evidence ledger and factual restraint is required. For explicit fiction, it means consistent with the premise and continuity ledger; invention is allowed unless it functions as a disguised allegation. Emotional indirectness remains required in either branch.

## Ten mechanisms

1. **Quantitative cold open.** When a number, duration, interval, or count naturally creates tension, it can open the piece. Evidence-bound work uses only confirmed or clearly attributed figures; fiction uses canon- or continuity-consistent figures. Avoid false precision.
2. **Concrete evidence.** Prefer ledger-supported objects, messages, receipts, schedules, gestures, and spatial details to abstract judgment.
3. **Restrained first person.** Report what “我” observed, did, remembered, or could not confirm; do not grant the narrator access to another person's mind.
4. **Compressed dialogue.** Keep only lines that cause, expose, or close a change. Evidence-bound work preserves wording supplied as exact or uses faithful indirect paraphrase within the workflow's factual bounds. Fiction may invent dialogue consistent with canon and continuity.
5. **Scale collision.** Set one large measure—years, money, distance, public attention—against one small ordinary detail.
6. **Recurring objects.** When the applicable ledger supports a suitable object, choose one primary motif and at most two supporting objects. Each return should alter meaning.
7. **Escalating chronology.** When events form an accumulating pattern, build through supported stages such as origin, access, accommodation, accumulation, rupture, and aftermath; compress or omit the rest.
8. **Strategic omission.** Leave silence around what is unknown or emotionally obvious. Omission is better than fabricated connective tissue.
9. **Circular ending.** When an organic return exists, revisit the opening measure, word, or object after its meaning has changed.
10. **Varied sentence rhythm.** Use longer sentences for accumulation and short sentences for turns; avoid a mechanical sequence of fragments.

## Quick reference

| Mechanism | Purpose | Misuse to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Quantitative cold open | Establish scale and tension quickly | Invented precision or number stacking |
| Concrete evidence | Make emotion inferable and traceable | Decorative props unsupported by the applicable ledger |
| Restrained first person | Limit knowledge and preserve credibility | Stating another person's hidden motive |
| Compressed dialogue | Make a turn audible | Unsourced real-person dialogue or continuity-breaking fictional dialogue |
| Scale collision | Put emotional cost into proportion | Forced grandeur or luxury-name dumping |
| Recurring objects | Carry continuity and changed meaning | Adding many symbolic objects |
| Escalating chronology | Give pressure a legible rise | Evidence-branch invention or mechanically filling every stage |
| Strategic omission | Respect uncertainty and create restraint | Withholding a supplied fact needed for sense |
| Circular ending | Produce closure without over-explaining | Repeating the opening unchanged |
| Varied sentence rhythm | Control pace and emphasis | Uniform short sentences or ornamental run-ons |

## Original micro-example

The following is an original, explicitly fictional micro-example. Its numbers are given within the example; they are not verified external facts. It illustrates the mechanisms rather than serving as a reusable template:

> 七年前，那张车票是27.5元。我后来给它买了一个48元的透明票套，像是纸比路程更值得保存。现在车站已经刷手机进站，旧票留在抽屉里，只记着那趟车晚点11分钟。十一分钟够我改签，却没让那个人回来。

Within this fictional example, the given figures do the work: the cheap ticket and costlier holder create scale collision, the ticket changes from access tool to residue, and the final 11-minute echo closes the scene. Do not borrow its wording for unrelated drafts.

## Common mistakes

- **Luxury-name dumping:** Brand, hotel, car, wine, travel, or staff details are not cinematic when unsupported.
- **Melodrama:** Replace declarations of devastation with observable choices, absences, or altered routines.
- **Metaphor overload:** Keep only imagery that clarifies an object or action supported by the applicable ledger.
- **Copied catchphrases:** Remove recognizable verbal signatures and write an original sentence logic.
- **Uniform short sentences:** Reserve fragments for genuine turns; vary pace elsewhere.
- **Invented specificity:** In factual, autobiographical, or identifiable-real-person material, do not add weather, lighting, décor, gestures, dialogue, timing, or inner states simply because they seem plausible. In explicit fiction, such specificity is permitted when internally consistent with premise and continuity and not a disguised allegation about an identifiable real person.

## Tone check

Favor calm observation, exact nouns, temporal markers, and controlled repetition. Remove redundant explanations after an image, action, or silence has already made the emotional point.
