# Rewrite Workflow

## 1. Classify the material

Decide whether the request concerns:

- **Factual or real-person material.**
- **Autobiographical material.**
- **Explicit fiction.**

Then follow the corresponding branch:

- **Factual or autobiographical:** Apply strict traceability and attribution. Preserve the narrator's account as testimony, mark uncertainty, and limit claims about other people.
- **Explicit fiction:** Invention may develop the declared premise as long as it remains consistent with the premise and continuity. If the fiction functions as disguised allegations about identifiable real people, treat it as factual/real-person material instead and do not invent.

Ask one focused question only when the answer would change factual integrity in the evidence branch, premise or continuity in the fiction branch, point of view, or the core turn. Otherwise shorten or omit unsupported evidence-bound material; in fiction, invent consistently or omit.

## 2. Build the applicable ledger

Use the evidence fact ledger for factual, autobiographical, and identifiable-real-person material. Use the fiction continuity ledger for explicit fiction. Keep the applicable ledger internal unless the user asks to see it. In later sections, “ledger-supported” means supported by whichever ledger applies.

### Evidence fact ledger

| Class | Meaning | Draft rule |
|---|---|---|
| Confirmed | Documented or independently corroborated material; “supplied” alone does not make it confirmed | May state plainly within the established scope, without embellishing beyond the record |
| Attributed | A claim assigned to a person, document, or other source | Retain the attribution and do not strengthen certainty |
| First-person account/testimony | The narrator's supplied account of personal observation or experience that is not independently corroborated | Write within the narrator's perspective and preserve the limits of that testimony |
| Uncertain | Memory, timing, or interpretation the source marks as doubtful | Preserve the uncertainty in the prose |
| Exact quote | Verbatim words supplied as exact | Quote exactly; do not extend or polish inside quotation marks |
| Paraphrase | Supplied meaning without exact wording | Render faithfully as indirect speech; never add scope, certainty, or implication |
| Forbidden invention | Unsupported real-person conduct, quote, amount, date, relationship, medical detail, motive, brand, location, witness, or corroboration | Omit, ask one essential question, or offer a genuinely fictional alternative; never draft it as fact |

For real-person conduct, a supplied claim remains attributed testimony unless documentation or independent corroboration establishes it. First-person prose may embody attribution through the narrator's perspective—“我看见” or “我记得”—but must not turn that testimony into external certainty elsewhere in the piece.

**Faithful paraphrase** preserves supplied meaning, scope, uncertainty, and attribution without adding implications. **Figurative language** must be unmistakably nonliteral and add no real-world action, setting, timing, dialogue, motive, or evidence.

For factual or real-person material, reconstruction may rearrange or synthesize only sourced substance. It must add no conduct, semantic claim, setting, chronology, dialogue content, or motive. Make the reconstruction and its uncertainty visible, and prefer faithful indirect paraphrase for allegations rather than recreated dialogue.

### Fiction continuity ledger

| Class | Meaning | Draft rule |
|---|---|---|
| User-established canon/premise | Characters, relationships, setting, events, rules, and tone given by the user | Preserve them unless the user requests a deliberate change |
| Permitted invention | New fictional detail not fixed by canon | May invent when it fits the premise, point of view, tone, and existing causal logic |
| Continuity constraints | Established timeline, knowledge, objects, character choices, and consequences | Check new scenes and dialogue for internal consistency |
| Prohibited disguised allegations | Fictionalized claims that remain recognizable as allegations about identifiable real people | Do not invent; reclassify as factual/real-person material, omit, or make the fiction genuinely non-identifying |

## 3. Find the narrative spine

Treat these stages as a menu for a possible spine, not a quota. Select only those organically supported by the ledger and omit the rest:

1. Opening tension.
2. Origin or access.
3. Accommodations and accumulating cost.
4. First refusal, absence, discovery, or changed word.
5. Aftermath.
6. Unresolved question or final image.

Use chronological escalation only when the supported events form an accumulating pattern. Braid time only when moving between periods changes the meaning of a ledger-supported detail, not merely to make the article appear complex.

## 4. Choose a contrast

If the ledger organically supports contrast, map possibilities such as large/small, public/private, crowded/empty, expensive/ordinary, moving/still, or recorded/forgotten. Select at most one central contrast; omit this mechanism when it would feel imposed.

## 5. Map the motif

If a ledger-supported object naturally changes meaning, select one primary motif and trace only the supported stages:

1. Neutral first appearance.
2. Intimate or practical use.
3. Its state at the rupture.
4. Its changed meaning at the ending.

Use at most two supporting objects. If no object evolves naturally, omit the motif. Do not add luxury objects or scenery to make the piece feel prestigious or cinematic.

## 6. Draft

Select from these moves only when the ledger organically supports them; do not force all six into every draft:

- Open on a ledger-supported contrast when one naturally exists: confirmed or clearly attributed in evidence-bound work, canon- or continuity-consistent in fiction.
- Ground scenes in ledger-supported concrete logistics.
- Use sparse, causal dialogue: evidence-bound work requires exact wording or faithful indirect paraphrase; fiction may invent dialogue consistent with canon and continuity.
- Establish a pattern and its break when both are supported.
- Use only ledger-supported aftermath objects, actions, and absences.
- End with a changed echo when the material offers one.

When evidence-bound material cannot sustain long-form treatment without invention, deliver a shorter piece. Explicit fiction may add internally consistent detail instead.

## 7. Audit

### Evidence traceability and attribution

Apply this audit only to factual, autobiographical, and identifiable-real-person material:

- Trace every concrete assertion to the ledger.
- Preserve attribution, uncertainty, quotation status, and reconstruction labels.
- Remove plausible but unsupported action, setting, chronology, dialogue, motive, and interiority.
- Ensure rumor has not become narrative fact through confident syntax or accumulated detail.

### Fiction premise and continuity

Apply this audit instead to explicit fiction:

- Preserve user-established canon and any requested premise changes.
- Trace new detail to permitted invention and check timeline, point of view, character knowledge, objects, dialogue, and consequences for continuity.
- Remove contradictions and accidental shifts in causal logic.
- Ensure the work does not function as disguised allegations about identifiable real people.

### Style mechanics

- Check emotional indirectness, concrete detail, restrained point of view, and varied rhythm. For each chosen contrast, motif, escalation, or circular echo, confirm that it arises organically from the applicable ledger; fewer mechanisms are valid.
- Cut explanations that repeat what an object, action, silence, or word change already shows.
- Remove luxury-name dumping, melodrama, ornamental metaphor, and excess motifs.

### De-imitation

- Remove borrowed or near-borrowed phrases, metaphors, catchphrases, and scene order.
- Replace the source's recognizable architecture with one derived from this material's own evidence or fictional canon.
- Remove source-specific clusters of luxury, travel, staff, medical, or relationship details; do not recreate those patterns with substituted names.

## 8. Deliver

Return the polished article only unless the user asks for the ledger or outline. If publication risk remains—such as unresolved attribution, uncertain quotation status, or a potentially identifying reconstruction—append a brief, separate verification note rather than interrupting the prose with process commentary.
